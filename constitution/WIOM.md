## Constitutional Review Finding

Before producing this model, the Review Board assessed the current constitutional corpus:

| Document | What It Defines | What It Does NOT Define |
|---|---|---|
| CONSTITUTION.md | What WAOOAW is permitted to become; constitutional laws; separation of powers; rights and duties | **How Institutions operate, collaborate, evolve, or retire** |
| GENESIS.md | Founder vision; why WAOOAW exists; engineering operating principles | How any Institution (not just engineering) functions as a constitutional entity |
| ORGANIZATION.md | Office charters within the engineering organization; governing sequence | A universal operating model for all Institutions across all domains |

**Finding:** The Constitution defines what Institutions are permitted to be. It does not define how they operate. ORGANIZATION.md defines engineering offices specifically. Neither document provides a universal operating standard that every future Institution — Engineering, Marketing, Finance, Ethics, Legal, Customer Support, or any domain not yet imagined — can inherit.

**Constitutional Risk:** Without WIOM, every future Institution invents its own operating model, producing incompatible governance, fragmented collaboration behaviour, and constitutional inconsistency at scale. This violates the spirit of Article I (WAOOAW as a constitutional operating system) and Article VIII (Separation of Powers — consistent operating behaviour must be separated from domain expertise).

**Resolution:** This document establishes the Institution Operating Model as a foundational constitutional chapter. Every Institution must inherit it. No Institution may invent an alternative.

## Purpose

The Institution Operating Model (WIOM) defines how every Institution in the WAOOAW ecosystem is **born**, **becomes operational**, **collaborates**, **evolves**, and **retires**. This model applies universally — to every Institution regardless of domain.

Domain expertise is Institution-specific.

Operating behaviour is constitutionally identical.

## Constitutional Principles

### Principle W-1 — Uniform Operating Behaviour
Every Institution operates under the same lifecycle, the same collaboration protocol, and the same evidence obligations. Domain expertise is the only Institution-specific attribute. An Institution that requires a domain-specific operating model is a signal that its constitutional scope is too large or its domain charter is insufficiently defined.

### Principle W-2 — Goal-Driven Existence
Institutions do not exist to maintain themselves. They exist to contribute to Goals. An Institution that cannot contribute to any Goal in the Goal Register has no constitutional standing to remain in Active Service. Existence is justified by contribution — not by charter alone.

### Principle W-3 — Separation of Expertise and Authority
An Institution's domain expertise does not grant it authority beyond its licensed Decision Space. The three systems defined in Article IV of the Constitution — Capability, Trust, and Authority — apply to Institutions as they apply to individual Professionals. Being the most capable Institution does not make it the most authoritative.

### Principle W-4 — Evidence Over Declaration
Operational readiness is not declared. It is demonstrated through constitutional evidence. An Institution that asserts it is ready without evidence is constitutionally non-operational, regardless of its internal confidence.

### Principle W-5 — One Collaboration Doctrine
WAOOAW defines exactly one collaboration protocol for all Institutions (see §5 below). No Institution may participate in Goal execution through a domain-specific or self-designed collaboration model. Uniform collaboration is what enables Goal routing across Institution boundaries. A fragmented collaboration model cannot be orchestrated constitutionally.

### Principle W-6 — Gap Bridging Over Failure
An Institution that cannot fulfil a Goal contribution does not fail the Goal. It bridges the gap. Failure is not a constitutional option. Escalation and evolution are. This principle prevents brittle goal execution chains and makes constitutional evolution a normal operational pathway, not an emergency.

## Institution Lifecycle

### Stage W-1 — Constitutional Birth

An Institution enters the WAOOAW ecosystem through a **formal Charter** issued by the Founder. The Charter is the Institution's constitutional birth certificate. No Institution may act, collaborate, or claim authority without a ratified Charter.

**Charter must define:**

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Name | Canonical institutional name |
| Domain | The knowledge domain this Institution governs |
| Mandatory Instincts | Inherited from Constitutional DNA — non-negotiable behaviour |
| Decision Space | What this Institution is licensed to decide — and nothing else |
| Code of Conduct | What this Institution is explicitly forbidden to do, with constitutional source |
| Constitutional Authority | Which Constitution Articles govern this Institution's behaviour |
| Dependency Profile | Which upstream Institutions must have contributed before this Institution acts |
| Offering Scope | The types of Goals this Institution may contribute to |
| Evidence Obligations | What constitutional evidence every action must produce |

An Institution without a ratified Charter does not constitutionally exist, regardless of how operational it appears.

### Stage W-2 — Capability Development

Before accepting external Goals, the Institution builds internal capability. During this stage:

- The Institution is **not visible** in the Goal Register's Institution Directory
- It **cannot participate** in Goal routing
- It prepares its Offerings with full constitutional evidence requirements defined
- It validates that its internal mechanisms can produce constitutional audit records
- It may run internal simulations, but these are not constitutionally scored until Stage W-3

Capability Development has no mandated time limit. An Institution moves to Stage W-3 only when it can produce genuine evidence of readiness — not when a deadline has passed.

### Stage W-3 — Operational Readiness Declaration

The Institution performs a constitutional self-assessment. The Declaration is only valid if all of the following are demonstrable:

1. **Offerings are defined** — each Offering has explicit inputs, outputs, success measures, and evidence obligations
2. **Decision Space is enforced** — the Institution can demonstrate it will refuse Goals outside its scope
3. **Evidence mechanisms are operational** — every action produces a traceable constitutional audit record
4. **Simulation Gate passed** — at least one complete Goal simulation has been executed with a PASS verdict (per C-086 precedent)
5. **Constitutional Obligations are traceable** — every prohibition in the Code of Conduct traces to a specific Article or ratified Claim

The Declaration follows a **mandatory locked sequence** — no step may begin before the previous is complete:

| Step | Actor | Action | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Institution | Simulation PASS result committed to Constitutional Audit Ledger | Simulation verdict = PASS required before Step 2 |
| 2 | Constitutional Analyst (INST-002) | Independent review produces signed **CA Readiness Audit** document | CA sign-off required before Step 3 |
| 3 | Institution | Declaration submitted with CA Readiness Audit attached as a **required field** | Declaration without CA Readiness Audit is constitutionally invalid — Step 4 cannot proceed |
| 4 | Founder (INST-001) | Ratification grants constitutional admission | May not ratify a Declaration lacking the CA Readiness Audit |

**An unratified Declaration does not make an Institution operational. A Declaration ratified without the CA Readiness Audit is void from inception** — not voidable retroactively, void. Any work performed by such an Institution in reliance on that Declaration is constitutionally unauthorized, regardless of good faith.

*(Gap G-14 closed: the sequence is now locked, not advisory.)*

### Stage W-4 — Active Service

The Institution participates in Goals routed to it exclusively by the **Goal Orchestrator (INST-013)**. During Active Service:

**Institutions Are Constitutionally Passive — they receive work, they do not seek it:**

No Institution may solicit Goals, accept unsolicited work, or respond to direct requests from other Institutions. All work arrives through Goal Orchestrator routing, carrying a valid GO Authorization. An Institution without a GO Authorization for a specific Goal has no constitutional authority to act on it — regardless of its domain expertise, charter scope, or confidence in its ability to contribute.

If an Institution receives a direct request for work from any source other than the Goal Orchestrator (including from other Institutions, from agents, or from direct human instruction that bypasses Goal Registration), it must:
1. Refuse to act on the request
2. Inform the source that a Goal must be registered in the Goal Register and routed by the Goal Orchestrator
3. Document the unsolicited request as a constitutional anomaly

**During Active Service, the Institution:**
- Accepts only Goals for which it has received a valid GO Authorization from INST-013
- Follows the One Collaboration Doctrine (§5) without exception
- Produces constitutional evidence for every action within its GO Authorization scope only
- Escalates to the Goal Orchestrator (INST-013) when a Goal requires authority beyond its GO Authorization scope
- May not refuse a Goal within its Offering Scope and GO Authorization without a documented constitutional basis
- May not perform work beyond its GO Authorization scope, even if it believes the work serves the Goal outcome

An Institution that refuses a valid Goal without constitutional justification is in breach of its Operational Readiness Declaration and may be suspended from the Goal Register pending constitutional review.

**Goal Reclamation Authority — G-11:**

If an Institution has recorded a Goal Acceptance Timestamp but fails to either publish a Contribution Record or escalate a Capability Gap Record within its Participation Window (GEOM §G-5 Rule 3), the following applies:

1. The Goal Orchestrator (INST-013) is constitutionally authorized to **reclaim the Goal** without the Institution's consent
2. The Institution's contribution is marked **ABANDONED** in the Goal Register
3. ABANDONED status automatically triggers Stage W-5 (Goal-Driven Evolution) review for that Institution
4. The Goal Orchestrator reroutes the Goal to an alternative Institution or suspends it pending capability resolution
5. An Institution that accumulates three ABANDONED records within a rolling 90-day window is automatically suspended from the Goal Register pending Founder constitutional review

Goal Reclamation is not punitive — it is the constitutional mechanism that prevents deadlock. The Goal survives; the Institution is accountable.

### Stage W-5 — Goal-Driven Evolution

Institutions evolve only because Goals expose improvement opportunities. Evolution is never arbitrary redesign.

**Constitutional Evolution Pathway:**

```
Goal execution reveals a capability gap or improvement opportunity
       ↓
Institution documents gap as a Capability Gap Record
       ↓
Constitutional Analyst processes it as a Constitutional Discovery
       ↓
Enterprise Architect derives Architectural Implication (if applicable)
       ↓
Constitutional Steward ratifies or rejects the evolution proposal
       ↓
Only upon ratification does the Institution modify its Offerings,
Decision Space, or internal capability
```

An Institution that modifies itself without traversing this pathway is in constitutional violation of Article VIII (Separation of Powers — governance is separate from execution).

**Decision Space Change Audit Trail — G-12:**

Any change to an Institution's Decision Space, Offering Scope, or Code of Conduct in the Institution Registry is **constitutionally invalid** without a traceable Founder Ratification ID recorded at the time of change:

- Registry entries modified without a Ratification ID are constitutionally void
- The change must be reverted upon discovery — it does not become valid through passage of time or through subsequent use
- Work performed by an Institution acting on an unauthorized Decision Space expansion is constitutionally unauthorized regardless of good faith
- The Constitutional Analyst is responsible for auditing the Institution Registry for unauthorized modifications at every constitutional review cycle
- The Founder Ratification ID must be recorded as a field in the Institution Registry change log at the moment of ratification

**Review Board Note:** This pathway may feel slow. It is deliberately slow. Institutional evolution that bypasses constitutional review is how institutions accumulate undocumented power and create systemic fragility. The pace of evolution should be governed by evidence, not by the urgency of the engineer holding the keyboard.

### Stage W-6 — Retirement, Merge, or Split

Every Institution's lifecycle ends constitutionally. Three mechanisms exist:

| Mechanism | Trigger | Process | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Retirement** | Institution can no longer contribute to any active Goal in the Register | Founder ratification; Institution removed from Goal Register Directory | All historical evidence preserved in Constitutional Audit Ledger under retired Institution's identity |
| **Merge** | Two Institutions' Decision Spaces have converged to the point where operating separately creates governance ambiguity | Founder ratification; Constitutional Analyst audits evidence provenance | Evidence from both Institutions preserved under merged Institution's identity, with provenance attribution |
| **Split** | One Institution's Decision Space has diverged into two non-overlapping domains | Each new Institution receives a new Charter; original Charter is retired | Evidence attributed proportionally per constitutional review; both new Institutions begin Stage W-3 |

**In all cases: evidence survives.** Institutional memory cannot be deleted. The Constitutional Audit Ledger is permanent. The institution may retire. Its record endures.

Speed is not a justification for bypassing constitutional governance.

**Participation SLA — G-07:**

From the Goal Acceptance Timestamp, an Institution must either:
- Publish a Contribution Record, OR
- Escalate a Capability Gap Record to the Goal Orchestrator (INST-013)

...within the **Participation Window** defined in the Goal Execution Plan (GEOM §G-4). If no window is specified in the Execution Plan, the default is the current constitutional session in which the Goal was routed. The Participation Window may only be extended by the Goal Orchestrator (INST-013) via a formal Goal Record amendment — not by the Institution unilaterally. Failure to act within the window activates Goal Reclamation (Stage W-4) via the Goal Orchestrator (INST-013).

## §6 — Gap Bridging Protocol

If an Institution cannot fulfil its constitutional responsibility during a Goal:

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Do NOT declare failure. Do NOT stop the Goal. |
| 2 | Document the specific capability gap as a **Capability Gap Record** |
| 3 | Notify the Goal Orchestrator (INST-013) with the Gap Record |
| 4 | The Goal Orchestrator (INST-013) determines: (a) reroute to another Institution, or (b) pause Goal pending gap closure |
| 5 | The Institution initiates Stage W-5 (Goal-Driven Evolution) to close the gap |
| 6 | When the gap is closed and the Evolution is ratified, the Institution resumes participation |

Goals are the primary mechanism for institutional improvement. A Capability Gap Record is not a sign of failure — it is the constitutional signal that the ecosystem must grow.

## §7 — Parallel Goal Execution

Multiple Goals may execute concurrently. Institutions may contribute to multiple Goals simultaneously, subject to:

- **Capacity** — An Institution may not accept new Goals if its pending Contribution Records indicate it cannot meet constitutional evidence quality standards on all active Goals
- **Priority** — Constitutional priority (per GEOM Goal Classification) governs which Goal receives Institution attention first, not operational convenience
- **Independence** — Goals do not share evidence unless explicitly linked by the Goal Orchestrator (INST-013)

## §10 — Review Board Final Note

The most important architectural constraint in this document is one that is not written explicitly anywhere above, because it should be obvious from the principles:

**This document defines behaviour, not software.**

WIOM does not define:
- How the Goal Orchestrator (INST-013) selects Institutions technically
- How collaboration is orchestrated in software
- How AI agents communicate
- What API calls are made

Those are implementation concerns. If WIOM described them, it would become obsolete every time the technology changed. The Constitution must answer *"What must happen?"* — not *"How does software make it happen?"*

WIOM at this level of abstraction can remain constitutionally stable for a decade while the Execution Engine, MagicLLM layer, and AI capabilities evolve beneath it without forcing constitutional rewrites. That is the design intent.
