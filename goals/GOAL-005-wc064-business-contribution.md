## Commercial Operating Model

WAOOAW sells accountable business capability, not access to AI technology. A customer hires a
declared constitutional professional to pursue a stated business outcome within an understood
goal, skill, authority, resource, and commercial envelope. Commercial governance therefore begins
before publication or hiring and continues through renewal, exception handling, and institutional
learning without converting the Founder into the approver of every granular choice.

The operating model has six business stages:

1. **Define the customer outcome.** State the measurable customer result, the professional role,
   the declared skills that contribute to it, and the limits that prevent an overstated promise.
2. **Compose the offering.** Bind one approved professional version to a customer-goal envelope,
   skill composition, resource envelope, included budgets, customer price, policy version, and
   required disclosures without creating new financial or lifecycle truth.
3. **Assess commercial defensibility.** Compare the promised value with expected resources,
   attributable costs, price, obligations, assumptions, and uncertainty while preserving all
   constitutional and commercial floors.
4. **Choose a governed disposition.** Produce one of `ALLOW`, `ALLOW_CALCULATED_RISK`, `REVISE`,
   `ESCALATE`, or `BLOCK`, with a reason, evidence basis, validity period, and review condition.
5. **Preserve customer choice.** Apply customer-impacting commercial changes prospectively with
   notice, review, choice, and active-period price protection; disclose provisional or disputed
   facts honestly.
6. **Learn without rewriting the profession.** Review patterns and outcomes to improve policy and
   future compositions. Changes to skills, Decision Space, prompts, or professional versions are
   proposals to the agent-lifecycle owner, never direct commercial-governance edits.

Autonomy is valid only inside a current approved policy envelope. The Founder governs policy,
consequential exceptions, and performance patterns. WAOOAW may make evidence-backed granular
adjustments when the applicable policy permits them, all floors remain preserved, customer impact
remains acceptable, and the risk is visible, bounded, expiring, and reviewable.

## Hireable-Offering Semantics

### Canonical Business Meaning

A **hireable offering** is a version-pinned commercial promise that an eligible constitutional
professional, using a declared composition of approved skills within a stated customer-goal and
resource envelope, can pursue measurable customer value at a disclosed price and included-budget
position under a current commercial policy.

It is not the professional itself, a skill catalogue entry, a price row, a projection, or a
publication page. It is the governed composition of those owner-controlled facts into a promise a
customer may responsibly evaluate and hire.

### Required Business Semantics

| Concept | Business meaning |
|---|---|
| Approved professional | The professional identity and version are eligible for the contemplated offer according to the lifecycle owner; commercial governance does not confer that eligibility |
| Declared skill composition | Only declared, approved skills needed for the customer outcome are included; ad-hoc capability is not implied |
| Customer-goal envelope | The measurable outcome, KPI family, scope, assumptions, exclusions, review horizon, and conditions under which honest limitation disclosure is required |
| Resource envelope | The categories and bounds of professional effort, provider usage, included budgets, advertising resources, and contingent resources available to pursue the goal |
| Commercial composition | Customer price, included budgets, expected attributable cost, assumptions, policy basis, and planning margins kept semantically distinct |
| Customer promise | The outcome pursued and service obligations undertaken, never an unsupported guarantee of the outcome itself |
| Offerability decision | A current evidenced disposition about whether the composition may proceed to publication or hiring; it is not permanent permission |
| Evidence reference | The durable basis needed to reconstruct why the disposition was made without treating a projection as settled truth |

### Hireable Conditions

An offering is hireable only when all of the following business conditions hold:

- the professional version and declared skills are eligible for the contemplated customer and
  goal;
- the promised outcome and KPI family are specific enough to evaluate customer value;
- capabilities, limitations, exclusions, price, included budgets, and material customer impact
  can be disclosed before hiring;
- expected resources and attributable costs are sufficiently current and complete for the
  applicable policy to assess them;
- the composition preserves constitutional floors, the approved commercial floor, active-period
  price protection, and the customer's rights;
- one current, reconstructable offerability disposition exists and its review or expiry condition
  has not been breached; and
- uncertainty, provisional facts, assumptions, and unresolved owner states are visible rather
  than replaced with optimistic defaults.

An offering is not hireable when required owner evidence is absent, stale, contradictory, or
unavailable; when a floor is breached; when evidence cannot be preserved; when the professional or
skill composition is ineligible; or when the promise cannot be stated honestly. Founder attention
may resolve an escalation or policy question, but it does not turn a constitutional or commercial
floor breach into an allow decision.

## Commercial Policy And Risk Semantics

### Policy Model

A commercial policy is a versioned, effective-dated and expiring expression of the Founder-approved
commercial intent under which WAOOAW may compose and assess offerings. It defines eligible classes
of adjustment, protected floors, risk dimensions, review obligations, escalation boundaries,
customer-impact obligations, and the evidence required to use the policy. Numeric values are
Founder-reserved policy inputs and are intentionally not set in this contribution.

| Policy concept | Required semantic rule |
|---|---|
| Scope | Identifies the offering, professional class, customer class, goal class, resource class, and period to which the policy applies |
| Floors | Constitutional rights and obligations are absolute; approved commercial floors are non-discretionary within the policy |
| Adjustment authority | Names which goal, resource, markup, included-budget, trial, or promotional adjustments may be made autonomously and which require revision or escalation |
| Evidence sufficiency | Requires current, attributable, confidence-qualified owner evidence; omission never becomes a favorable default |
| Customer impact | Requires notice, review, choice, grandfathering, disclosure, and remedy semantics appropriate to the prospective change |
| Validity | Has a version, effective date, review cadence, expiry condition, and supersession behavior |
| Accountability | Preserves the policy owner, decision owner, assumptions, reasons, evidence references, and observed outcome for later review |

### Risk Model

**Calculated risk** means a policy-permitted departure from the preferred baseline, taken to
preserve or improve customer value where no floor is breached and uncertainty remains bounded and
honestly represented. It is not a waiver, an undocumented exception, a way to spend beyond
authority, or permission to conceal a weak offering.

Risk must be characterized across these business dimensions without fixing numeric thresholds:

- customer-outcome risk: likelihood that the resource composition cannot support the stated goal;
- commercial risk: likelihood that attributable cost or customer economics move outside the
  preferred policy position while remaining above every protected floor;
- exposure concentration: accumulation by offering, cohort, customer, resource class, and period;
- evidence risk: freshness, completeness, confidence, contradiction, and provisional status;
- customer-impact risk: effect on price, included value, expectations, choice, or remedy; and
- reversibility and duration: ability to revise the composition and the period before mandatory
  review or expiry.

### Disposition Semantics

| Disposition | Business meaning |
|---|---|
| `ALLOW` | The composition is defensible within the preferred policy position and all required evidence, floors, and customer protections are satisfied |
| `ALLOW_CALCULATED_RISK` | The composition remains within the permitted risk envelope and all floors, but identified assumptions or exposure require explicit expiry and review |
| `REVISE` | A different goal, resource, price, included-budget, trial, or promotional composition could become defensible without changing protected policy |
| `ESCALATE` | A consequential exception, ambiguity, concentration, or policy decision belongs to a named owner and cannot be decided autonomously |
| `BLOCK` | A protected floor, eligibility rule, evidence obligation, or customer-right condition fails; publication and hiring must not proceed |

Unknown, stale, unavailable, contradictory, disputed, provisional, expired, and superseded are
states of the evidence or decision, not alternate ways to say `ALLOW`. A reusable permission must
fail closed when any state invalidates the basis on which it was issued.

### Cross-Agent Reuse Rules

Skills are reusable business assets, but an offering is not reusable merely because two
professionals share a skill name. Reuse must preserve the independence of capability, trust, and
authority and must not erase domain specialization.

1. **Reuse the capability meaning.** A reusable capability has a stable verb-object business
   purpose, declared inputs and outcome contribution, limitations, evidence obligation, and cost
   ingredients that remain meaningful across more than one professional.
2. **Recompose for the profession.** Each professional version declares which approved reusable
   capabilities it possesses and how they contribute to its domain-specific customer goal. Reuse
   does not grant an undeclared skill or enlarge Decision Space.
3. **Reassess customer value.** Outcome fit, KPIs, vocabulary, resource assumptions, commercial
   policy, and disclosures are evaluated for each offering; another agent's prior allow decision
   is not portable permission.
4. **Keep trust and authority local.** Evidence of capability reuse may inform composition, but
   customer-specific trust and licensed authority do not transfer between professionals or
   customers.
5. **Attribute cost without copying truth.** Common cost categories and charging-unit semantics
   may be reused; each owner remains authoritative for current cost, usage, price, and settlement
   facts.
6. **Route learning as a proposal.** Cross-agent evidence may identify an improvement opportunity,
   but only the lifecycle owner may approve a skill, prompt, Decision Space, or version change.

The reuse decision has three business outcomes: `REUSE_AS_IS` when the capability meaning and
obligations remain unchanged; `REUSE_WITH_DOMAIN_COMPOSITION` when the common capability is valid
but goal, language, limits, evidence, or resources must be specialized; and `DO_NOT_REUSE` when
reuse would overstate capability, bypass approval, transfer authority, or obscure customer value.
